# Training research behind session mode

What the evidence says about building muscle and getting fit with bodyweight, and the rule DV Coach takes from each finding. Collected on 2026-10-04. The user asked for the best-supported choices rather than their own preferences.

Strength of evidence is marked as:
- **Strong:** a large meta-analysis or a position stand.
- **Moderate:** a smaller meta-analysis, or consistent trials.
- **Practice:** what coaches commonly do, with little direct research.

## Building muscle

| Topic | What the research says | Evidence | DV Coach rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any training beats none | ACSM's 2026 position stand (137 systematic reviews, 30,000+ people) says the biggest gain is going from no resistance training to some. Consistency matters more than the "perfect" plan, and bodyweight and home routines work. | Strong. [ACSM 2026](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years) | Keep sessions short and simple, and never punish a missed day. |
| How often | Train every major muscle at least twice a week (ACSM 2026). When weekly sets are equal, frequency barely changes muscle growth ([Schoenfeld 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/); Pelland 2026). | Strong | 2, 3 or 4 days a week, and every muscle is worked at least twice. |
| Weekly sets | Take about 10 or more sets per muscle a week (ACSM 2026). More sets keep adding growth, with smaller gains each time: 6 → 12 sets is a clear jump, and 18 → 24 still adds a little. Sets that work a muscle indirectly count as half ([Pelland et al. 2026, *Sports Medicine*](https://train.fitness/personal-trainer-blogs/not-all-sets-are-created-equal)). Even 4 sets a week works when time is short ([Iversen 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8449772/)). | Strong | Start near 10 sets per muscle per week, counting secondary muscles as half. Add sets as the person adapts, up to about 20. |
| Load and reps | Light, medium and heavy loads build similar muscle when sets are hard enough; heavy loads build more strength ([Lopez 2021](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/75929)). Push-ups matched to a 40% bench press grew chest and triceps as much as the bench press ([Kikuchi 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29541130/)). | Strong (load), moderate (push-ups) | Bodyweight is enough to grow. Main exercises use 6–15 reps; calves and core use 10–20. |
| Effort | Going to failure isn't necessary for strength or size (ACSM 2026; [Refalo 2023](https://dro.deakin.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/Influence_of_Resistance_Training_Proximity-to-Failure_on_Skeletal_Muscle_Hypertrophy_A_Systematic_Review_with_Meta-analysis/22030796)). Sets closer to failure grow muscle somewhat more, but not strength ([Robinson 2024](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-the-dose-response-relationship-between-estimated-resist/)). | Strong | Most sets stop with 1–3 reps left. |
| Judging effort | People guess they have about 1 rep fewer left than they really do, and they guess much worse in sets above 12 reps ([Halperin 2022](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/67)). | Moderate | The **last set of each exercise goes as far as good form allows**. It shows people what "1–2 left" really feels like, and gives the engine a real number every session. Exercises move up a level before reps get very high. |
| Rest | Resting more than 60 s gives a small growth benefit, and more than 90 s adds nothing ([Singer 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/)). Supersets roughly halve session time while keeping the same number of sets ([Iversen 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8449772/)). | Moderate | Exercises go in **pairs that use different muscles** (push with pull, legs with core), with 60 s between sets. Each muscle then gets about 2.5–3 minutes before its next set, and a session takes about half the time. The r/bodyweightfitness routine also uses pairs ([Recommended Routine](https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/recommended-routine)). |
| Rep speed | Reps lasting anywhere from 0.5 to 8 s build similar muscle; slower than 10 s is worse ([Schoenfeld 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25601394/)). | Moderate | Cue: lower for 2–3 s. A slow-lowering version (4 s down) bridges big jumps between levels. |
| Range of motion | Full range beats partial reps slightly. Training in the stretched position may grow more muscle ([Wolf 2023](https://doaj.org/article/99e86b317bec40f5abc2f0008358737e)). | Moderate | Cue the deep position: chest to the floor, back knee near the floor, a short pause at the bottom. |
| Progression without weights | The r/bodyweightfitness routine uses double progression on a ladder: add reps up to the top of the range, then move to a harder version and start again at the bottom ([Recommended Routine](https://www.liftosaur.com/programs/recommended-routine)). A progressive push-up ladder raised bench-press strength ([Kotarsky 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29466268/)). | Practice, plus a small trial | Each movement has a ladder of levels. Reps grow by 1 when the last session went well; at the top of the range, move up a level. |
| Rest weeks | A planned week off in the middle of 9 weeks didn't change muscle growth but slightly reduced strength gains ([Coleman 2024](https://peerj.com/articles/16777)). | Moderate (one trial) | No scheduled rest weeks. A rough week steps back one notch, and after 2+ weeks off you return with fewer reps. |
| Men and women | Muscle growth is similar, and women gain relatively more upper-body strength ([Roberts 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32218059/)). | Moderate | Same program for everyone. No gender setting, unlike the reference app. |
| Adjusting volume | The RP Hypertrophy app sets next week's sets from pump, soreness and workload feedback ([RP help](https://help.rpstrength.com/hc/en-us/articles/32600173777815-How-does-the-app-determine-when-to-add-weight-reps-and-sets)). | Practice | DV Coach uses signals it already collects or can measure: sets completed, effort ratings, last-set reps and the readiness check-in. One change per week. |

## Getting fit and losing fat

| Topic | What the research says | Evidence | DV Coach rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resistance training alone | Lowers body fat by about 1.5 percentage points compared with doing nothing ([Wewege 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34536199/)). | Strong | Strength sessions stay in the plan; they keep muscle while fat comes off. |
| Adding cardio | Cardio, alone or combined with strength training, loses about 1 kg more fat than strength training alone over 10+ weeks. Strength training keeps the most lean mass. When the energy burned is matched, all three are equal ([Lafontant 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12107660/)). | Moderate | The "get fit" plan combines strength sessions with short cardio circuits. |
| Does cardio cost muscle? | Combined training doesn't reduce muscle growth or maximal strength ([Schumann 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474354/)). | Strong | Safe to combine. |
| Intervals or steady cardio | Similar fat loss (about 2.6 kg each), and intervals take about 40% less time ([Wewege 2017](https://m.medicalxpress.com/news/2017-04-moderate-high-intensity-similar-results.html)). | Moderate | Bodyweight interval circuits of 10–20 minutes, with low-impact versions by default. |
| Short bursts | "Exercise snacks" of 5 minutes or less, done 2+ times a day, improved fitness in inactive adults, with 83% adherence ([exercise snacks review](https://athletechnews.com/exercise-snacks-can-boost-fitness-in-inactive-adults-study/)). The effect weakened after correcting for publication bias. | Moderate to weak | Fits the existing spread-out mode. Later: optional 2-minute "move" prompts on rest days. |
| Daily steps | The mortality benefit levels off around 8,000–10,000 steps a day under age 60, and 6,000–8,000 at 60+ ([Paluch 2022](https://hospitalhealthcare.com/specialisms/cardiovascular/mortality-benefit-from-higher-daily-steps-plateaus/)). | Strong (observational) | A step goal once Health Connect is added. |
| Food | Fat loss mostly comes from eating less. To keep muscle, lose about 0.5–1% of body weight a week ([Helms 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033492/)). Protein above about 1.6 g per kg per day adds no more muscle ([Morton 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)). | Strong | The coach explains these as general guidance. The app writes no meal plans and shows no calorie-burn estimates. The report shows the weekly weight trend against 0.5–1%. |

## What the research doesn't settle

These choices are ours, not the literature's:
- The exact rep ranges per exercise.
- The ladder order.
- The 60 s paired rest.
- Using the last set as a test.
- How fast volume ramps up.

They're starting values, to tune from real logs. The same caveat already applies to the spread-out mode.
